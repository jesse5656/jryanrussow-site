# Purpose

This document performs the first formal cross-case comparison in Research
Program 001.

The purpose is to determine which propositions recur independently across two
materially different organizations and operating environments.

The purpose is not to convert recurrence automatically into universal theory.

The comparison must preserve:

- case-specific language;
- technical differences;
- organizational-level differences;
- uncertainty;
- contrary evidence;
- contextual versus direct causes;
- and evidence that either organization sometimes functioned effectively.

---

# Comparison Boundary

The two cases differ substantially.

Challenger involved:

- a national space program;
- NASA and Morton Thiokol;
- flight-readiness and launch decisions;
- Solid Rocket Motor field-joint risk;
- formal launch constraints and waivers;
- and a discrete pre-launch decision sequence.

BP Texas City involved:

- a commercial refinery;
- long-duration process operations;
- refinery and corporate management;
- recurring process-safety deficiencies;
- maintenance, staffing, MOC, training, and instrumentation systems;
- and a startup operation culminating in loss of containment and explosion.

Similarity is therefore not assumed from industry, technology, or identical
organizational structure.

Only independently recurring organizational relationships are eligible for
cross-case findings.

# Cross-Case Findings

## CCF-01 — Institutional Knowledge Can Exist Before Failure Without Becoming Fully Effective at the Decision or Action Point

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CROSS-CASE FINDING

Both cases independently demonstrate substantial pre-event organizational
knowledge relevant to catastrophic risk.

In Challenger:

- CF-01 establishes relevant pre-51-L technical knowledge;
- CF-02 establishes that institutional possession did not produce common
  decision-level awareness;
- CF-04 establishes incomplete delivery of the risk picture to higher-level
  authority.

In BP Texas City:

- BF-01 establishes substantial pre-event process-safety knowledge;
- BF-02 establishes that known safety information did not reliably produce
  effective corrective action;
- BF-09 establishes that aggregate leadership response remained insufficient
  relative to the warning record.

The recurring relationship is therefore narrower than "the organization did
not know."

**Cross-case conclusion:**

Institutional possession of relevant knowledge is not equivalent to effective
availability, authority, or conversion of that knowledge at the point where
action is required.

**Qualification**

The cases do not establish that every relevant individual possessed every
piece of information.

The specific conversion failure differs between cases.

---

## CCF-02 — Information Can Lose Operational Significance as It Moves Through Organizational Systems

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CROSS-CASE FINDING

In Challenger:

- CF-03 establishes inconsistent preservation of O-ring risk significance;
- CF-02 and CF-04 show incomplete representation and escalation into important
  decision channels.

In BP Texas City:

- BF-03 establishes fragmented preservation of operational risk history;
- BF-04 establishes that personal-safety performance did not adequately
  represent process-safety risk;
- BF-02 establishes unreliable conversion of identified safety information
  into corrective action.

The cases differ in the mechanisms of distortion.

Challenger prominently involved changing representation across reporting and
readiness channels.

BP prominently involved fragmentation among reporting systems, incomplete
investigation, and safety measures that poorly represented catastrophic
process risk.

**Cross-case conclusion:**

Preserving the existence of information is insufficient if its severity,
meaning, context, or operational relevance is not preserved with it.

---

## CCF-03 — Formal Safety and Control Systems Do Not Guarantee Effective Independent Risk Control

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CROSS-CASE FINDING

Both organizations possessed formal safety, assurance, review, or control
structures.

In Challenger:

- CF-05 establishes that safety, reliability, and quality-assurance structures
  did not provide effective independent redundancy for the O-ring risk.

In BP Texas City:

- BF-07 establishes repeated ineffective implementation of Management of
  Change;
- BF-09 establishes an insufficient aggregate leadership and oversight response;
- BF-05 demonstrates persistence of known safety-critical equipment problems;
- the BP case nevertheless documents formal safety-management systems.

**Cross-case conclusion:**

The existence of formal control architecture is distinguishable from its
ability to independently detect, challenge, escalate, and interrupt unsafe
operations.

**Qualification**

The cases do not establish that formal safety systems are generally
ineffective.

The finding concerns failure of formal structure to guarantee effective
redundancy.

---

## CCF-04 — Repeated Successful Operation Can Coexist With Persistent Abnormal Conditions Without Forcing System Reassessment

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CROSS-CASE FINDING

In Challenger:

- CF-06 establishes repeated successful missions alongside O-ring erosion,
  blow-by, waivers, and increasing acceptance of degradation.

In BP Texas City:

- BF-10 establishes repeated abnormal startups, relief events, releases,
  equipment malfunctions, and prior incidents without sufficiently broad
  system reassessment;
- BF-03 documents weaknesses in preserving the complete incident history.

**Cross-case conclusion:**

Operational success between abnormal events does not itself demonstrate that
the underlying system is safe, and repeated abnormal outcomes may coexist with
continued operation without triggering sufficiently broad reassessment.

**Qualification**

This finding does not establish a universal psychological mechanism such as
"normalization of deviance."

The historical relationship is established.

The mechanism remains a later research question.

---

## CCF-05 — Operating Pressure Can Reduce Organizational Capacity Without Being Proven as the Sole or Direct Cause of a Catastrophic Decision

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CROSS-CASE FINDING

In Challenger:

- CF-07 establishes substantial flight-rate, workload, staffing, logistics,
  customer, and resource pressure;
- the Challenger record does not establish direct external coercion forcing
  the January 27–28 launch decision.

In BP Texas City:

- BF-08 establishes resource, production, staffing, maintenance, and
  organizational pressures that reduced process-safety capacity;
- BF-08 expressly does not establish cost cutting or production pressure as a
  sole cause.

**Cross-case conclusion:**

Operating pressure can reduce the time, staffing, attention, technical margin,
maintenance capacity, or organizational slack available for risk management
without needing to be the sole or direct cause of the final catastrophic
action.

---

## CCF-06 — Failure Frequently Occurred in Conversion and Follow-Through Rather Than in the Total Absence of Knowledge or Formal Process

**Status:** ESTABLISHED CROSS-CASE FINDING

Neither case supports a simple absence explanation.

Challenger had:

- substantial technical knowledge;
- formal readiness processes;
- safety and quality structures;
- launch constraints;
- engineering review mechanisms.

BP Texas City had:

- extensive process-safety knowledge;
- incident systems;
- PSM;
- MOC;
- training;
- mechanical-integrity programs;
- audits;
- safety governance.

Yet material risk remained insufficiently controlled.

**Cross-case conclusion:**

Across these two cases, critical weakness repeatedly occurred between
institutional possession or formal process and reliable organizational
conversion into effective control.

**Qualification**

This finding does not yet establish a universal mechanism called Selective
Conversion Failure.

It establishes only the recurring cross-case relationship.

## Selective Conversion Failure

The Challenger research program held Selective Conversion Failure as an
Institute-generated hypothesis.

Pass 19 provides meaningful support for parts of that hypothesis:

- relevant knowledge existed in both cases;
- formal processes existed in both cases;
- risk information was incompletely converted into effective control;
- escalation, representation, follow-through, or reassessment failed in
  important ways.

However, the strongest version of Selective Conversion Failure also expects
evidence that the same organization effectively converts comparable knowledge
elsewhere.

Challenger has explicit counterevidence through CF-08.

BP Texas City documents genuine corrective initiatives and functioning formal
systems, but the current BP findings do not establish a sufficiently clean
within-organization successful-hazard control for direct equivalence with
CF-08.

**Pass 19 decision:**

SELECTIVE CONVERSION FAILURE — SUPPORTED BUT NOT YET ESTABLISHED AS FRAMEWORK.

Pass 20 must decide whether the construct should:

- remain a hypothesis;
- be narrowed;
- be renamed;
- become a candidate framework proposition;
- or be rejected.
