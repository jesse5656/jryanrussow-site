---

# Purpose

Pass 18 attempts to defeat, narrow, merge, or reject the ten BP Texas City
findings established in Pass 17.

The purpose is not to defend the findings.

The purpose is to determine whether each finding survives deliberate scrutiny
against:

- contradictory evidence;
- evidentiary overlap;
- alternative explanations;
- organizational-level ambiguity;
- temporal ambiguity;
- retrospective-causality leakage;
- causal overstatement; and
- premature cross-case generalization.

---

# Validation Standard

A finding survives only when:

1. its supporting evidence remains materially sufficient;
2. controlling qualifications remain explicit;
3. it is distinguishable from adjacent findings;
4. it does not depend on uncertain technical details;
5. it does not convert contextual evidence into sole causation;
6. it preserves organizational attribution;
7. it remains BP-case-specific.

---

# Finding-by-Finding Adversarial Results

## BF-01 — Material Process-Safety Knowledge Existed Before the Explosion

**Result:** SURVIVES

The evidence of pre-event knowledge is broad and independently distributed
across prior incidents, audits, staffing assessments, training records,
equipment records, and safety-culture reviews.

The strongest limiting qualification is that institutional possession was
distributed and uneven.

The finding therefore remains valid only at the organizational level and does
not imply uniform individual knowledge.

---

## BF-02 — Known Safety Information Did Not Reliably Produce Corrective Action

**Result:** SURVIVES

The evidence includes repeated examples of incomplete, delayed, or ineffective
closure.

Contrary evidence shows that genuine corrective initiatives also occurred.

The finding survives because it states unreliability rather than total absence
of corrective action.

---

## BF-03 — Incident Reporting and Learning Did Not Preserve a Complete Operational Risk History

**Result:** SURVIVES

Appendix Q provides direct evidence that no single system contained the full
blowdown-event history and that significant events were split among safety,
environmental, operational, and other records.

This finding remains distinct from BF-10.

BF-03 concerns preservation and integration of risk history.

BF-10 concerns whether repeated abnormal outcomes triggered system
reassessment.

---

## BF-04 — Personal-Safety Performance Did Not Provide an Adequate View of Process-Safety Risk

**Result:** SURVIVES

The coexistence of record-low OSHA recordable injury performance and serious
process-safety deterioration directly defeats use of personal-injury metrics as
a sufficient proxy for catastrophic process risk.

The finding does not claim that personal-safety metrics were useless.

It states that they were insufficient for representing process-safety health.

---

## BF-05 — Mechanical-Integrity Systems Did Not Reliably Restore Known Safety-Critical Equipment

**Result:** SURVIVES

Multiple independent examples support the finding.

Instrument-specific causal certainty varies and remains qualified.

The finding is therefore systemic but does not depend on asserting identical
failure certainty for each instrument.

---

## BF-06 — Hazardous Startup Was Not Supported by Sufficiently Reliable Training, Staffing, and Technical Assistance

**Result:** SURVIVES

The combined record supports inadequacy in abnormal-situation preparation,
staffing support, training completion, role documentation, fatigue control, and
availability of experienced technical assistance.

The finding is not an operator-blame proposition.

Training existed, and fatigue remains only a likely contributor.

---

## BF-07 — Management of Change Did Not Reliably Control Technical and Organizational Change

**Result:** SURVIVES

A formal MOC system existed.

The record nevertheless shows repeated failure to invoke or apply it across
equipment, procedures, organizational arrangements, and trailer siting.

The finding remains an implementation finding rather than a policy-absence
finding.

---

## BF-08 — Resource, Production, and Organizational Pressures Reduced Process-Safety Capacity

**Result:** SURVIVES AS CONTEXTUAL FINDING

The evidence supports resource constraints, staffing pressure, production
pressure, organizational instability, and maintenance constraints.

The evidence also shows management resistance to some cuts and real safety
initiatives.

The finding therefore remains contextual and must not be treated as a
single-cause explanation.

---

## BF-09 — Leadership and Oversight Responses Were Insufficient Relative to the Warning Record

**Result:** SURVIVES

The warning record was broad and repeated.

Leadership responses existed but did not eliminate the major underlying
process-safety deficiencies before the event.

The finding must continue distinguishing:

- BP Board oversight;
- senior refining leadership;
- Texas City site management;
- supervisory management.

---

## BF-10 — Repeated Abnormal Outcomes Became Insufficient Triggers for System Reassessment

**Result:** SURVIVES

Recurring abnormal startups, relief events, vapor releases, equipment
malfunctions, and prior incidents were repeatedly followed by continued
operation without sufficiently broad system reassessment.

This finding remains distinct from BF-02 and BF-03:

- BF-02 concerns corrective conversion;
- BF-03 concerns preservation and integration of risk history;
- BF-10 concerns whether abnormal experience triggered reconsideration of the
  underlying system.

The finding does not require every historical event to be identical to the
March 2005 event.

---

# Adversarial Merger Review

No mandatory merger is justified.

The closest finding relationships are:

- BF-01 / BF-02 — knowledge possession versus conversion into action;
- BF-02 / BF-10 — corrective closure versus reassessment of the system itself;
- BF-03 / BF-10 — memory preservation versus learning-trigger effectiveness;
- BF-05 / BF-07 — equipment restoration versus change control;
- BF-06 / BF-08 — operating capability versus organizational/resource context;
- BF-08 / BF-09 — pressure environment versus leadership response.

Each pair remains analytically distinct.
