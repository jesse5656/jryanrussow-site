## Purpose

The Challenger case produced eight established findings and several
cross-case research questions.

The second case must test those questions independently.

The selection objective is therefore not:

> Which historical event resembles Challenger most?

It is:

> Which independently documented case provides the strongest opportunity to
> falsify, narrow, or corroborate the Challenger-derived hypotheses?

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Selection Criteria

Each candidate is scored from 0–5 on:

1. primary-source depth;
2. pre-event knowledge reconstruction;
3. organizational traceability;
4. independent failure anchor;
5. contradictory evidence;
6. time depth;
7. identifiable decision point;
8. comparative distance from Challenger;
9. within-institution control possibility;
10. source accessibility and preservability.

Maximum score: **50**

# Selection Decision

## Selected Second Case

**BP Texas City Refinery Explosion — March 23, 2005**

## Why It Wins

BP Texas City provides the strongest combined test of:

- knowledge existence;
- information distribution;
- risk representation;
- escalation;
- independent safety controls;
- anomaly and incident learning;
- organizational pressure;
- resource decisions;
- management systems;
- corrective action;
- organizational performance across multiple levels.

It also creates substantial comparative distance from Challenger.

The case occurs in:

- a private corporation rather than a government space program;
- continuous industrial processing rather than discrete space missions;
- petroleum refining rather than aerospace;
- a different regulatory structure;
- a different technical hazard environment.

This reduces the probability that recurrence would merely reflect
NASA-specific structure.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Why Davis-Besse Is Not Selected First

Davis-Besse scored only one point lower.

It is an exceptional research case and should be preserved as the first reserve.

BP Texas City is preferred because:

1. the institutional unit is somewhat cleaner;
2. the industrial setting provides greater distance from Challenger;
3. the CSB investigation explicitly examines corporate and site-level
   management systems;
4. the source archive contains extensive pre-event organizational evidence;
5. the case provides strong opportunities to test both organizational failure
   and successful corrective capacity.

Davis-Besse may become especially valuable as a third case because it adds
formal nuclear regulatory oversight to the comparison set.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Falsification Targets for BP Texas City

BP Texas City must not be researched as a search for Challenger parallels.

The investigation must test the following propositions independently.

## FT-01 — Knowledge absence

Was critical pre-event hazard knowledge actually absent?

If yes, CF-09 and Selective Conversion Failure are weakened.

## FT-02 — Decision availability

Did relevant knowledge reach management in complete, usable form?

If yes, CF-09 requires revision.

## FT-03 — Risk representation

Was process-safety significance represented accurately?

If yes, Challenger CF-03 may not generalize.

## FT-04 — Escalation

Did relevant concerns successfully escalate to people with authority?

If yes, Challenger CF-04 may be case-specific.

## FT-05 — Independent control

Did safety, audit, assurance, or regulatory structures possess sufficient
information and authority?

If they functioned effectively, CF-05 may not recur.

## FT-06 — Prior successful operation

Did successful operations alter treatment of recurring anomalies?

If no, CF-06 may be Challenger-specific.

## FT-07 — Institutional pressure

Were staffing, cost, production, or resource pressures present?

If present, were they contextual or directly causal?

## FT-08 — Selective performance

Did BP respond effectively to materially similar hazards elsewhere?

If no, Challenger CF-08 may not generalize and Selective Conversion Failure
loses an important component.
