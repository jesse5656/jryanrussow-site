---

# Research Result

The two completed cases provide substantial support for a recurring
organizational relationship:

> Organizations may possess materially relevant knowledge and formal control
> systems while still failing to convert that knowledge reliably into
> decision availability, escalation, corrective action, reassessment, or
> operational control.

This relationship appears independently in two materially different
organizations and technical environments.

That recurrence is meaningful.

It does not establish universality.

---

# Empirical Foundation

The empirical layer entering Pass 20 consists of six established cross-case
findings.

## CCF-01 — Knowledge Possession Versus Effective Decision / Action Availability

Institutional possession of relevant knowledge is not equivalent to effective
availability, authority, or conversion of that knowledge where action is
required.

## CCF-02 — Preservation of Operational Significance

Information can remain present while losing severity, meaning, context, or
operational relevance as it moves through organizational systems.

## CCF-03 — Formal Structure Versus Effective Control

Formal safety, assurance, review, or control structures do not guarantee
effective independent detection, challenge, escalation, or interruption of
unsafe operation.

## CCF-04 — Success Alongside Abnormal Conditions

Repeated successful operation can coexist with persistent abnormal conditions
without forcing sufficiently broad system reassessment.

## CCF-05 — Operating Pressure and Organizational Capacity

Schedule, resource, production, staffing, workload, or organizational
pressure can reduce risk-management capacity without being established as the
sole or direct cause of the final catastrophic action.

## CCF-06 — Conversion and Follow-Through

Critical organizational weakness can occur between institutional possession
or formal process and reliable conversion into effective control.

# Framework-Readiness Decision

## Decision

**NO ESTABLISHED INSTITUTIONAL MEMORY FRAMEWORK PROPOSITIONS ARE CREATED IN
PASS 20.**

The six cross-case findings are sufficiently supported to become inputs to a
candidate framework.

They are not promoted to universal or mature framework propositions.

Reasons:

1. only two historical cases have been completed;
2. both cases concern catastrophic organizational failure;
3. the sample does not yet test successful organizations systematically;
4. the cases do not establish prevalence;
5. the cases do not establish that the same mechanisms operate in every
   organizational environment;
6. some mechanisms remain case-specific;
7. independent field application has not yet tested the diagnostic usefulness
   of the proposed framework.

The appropriate maturity state is:

**EMPIRICALLY SUPPORTED CROSS-CASE FINDINGS → CANDIDATE FRAMEWORK INPUTS**

not:

**ESTABLISHED UNIVERSAL FRAMEWORK**

# Candidate Institutional Memory Framework

The research supports testing an organizational conversion chain.

## Candidate Conversion Chain

**Knowledge exists**

↓

**Knowledge enters institutional possession**

↓

**Information is preserved with sufficient meaning and context**

↓

**Information reaches the appropriate organizational authority**

↓

**Its significance is recognized**

↓

**Action is authorized**

↓

**Action is performed**

↓

**Action effectiveness is verified**

↓

**Experience updates organizational memory and future control**

This is a candidate analytical model.

It is not yet a proven universal sequence.

Organizations may contain additional stages, parallel routes, feedback loops,
or different authority structures.

# Candidate Diagnostic Domains

## D1 — Knowledge Existence

Does materially relevant knowledge exist somewhere within or available to the
organization?

## D2 — Institutional Possession

Where is the knowledge stored, documented, remembered, or embodied?

Does the organization possess it beyond one individual?

## D3 — Meaning Preservation

Does severity, uncertainty, context, and operational significance remain
attached to the information as it moves?

## D4 — Decision Availability

Does the information reach the people or systems with authority to act?

## D5 — Recognition and Interpretation

Does the receiving authority understand why the information matters?

## D6 — Escalation

What causes the issue to move to a higher level of authority?

What prevents escalation?

## D7 — Corrective Conversion

Does recognized knowledge produce a concrete corrective action?

## D8 — Execution

Is the authorized action actually performed?

## D9 — Verification

Does the organization verify that corrective action solved the underlying
problem rather than merely closing an item?

## D10 — Learning / Reassessment

Do repeated abnormalities, incidents, near misses, or unsuccessful corrections
cause reassessment of the underlying system?

## D11 — Formal-System Effectiveness

Do formal systems such as audits, MOC, training, safety, quality,
maintenance, reporting, and assurance function effectively in practice?

## D12 — Organizational Capacity

Do schedule, staffing, workload, budget, production, leadership turnover, or
organizational instability reduce the capacity to convert knowledge into
action?

# Selective Conversion Failure

## Current Status

**SUPPORTED HYPOTHESIS — NOT ESTABLISHED FRAMEWORK**

The completed research materially supports the possibility that organizations
can fail selectively at one or more conversion boundaries even when relevant
knowledge and formal systems exist.

The hypothesis is useful because it challenges simplistic explanations based
only on ignorance or absence of process.

The evidence does not yet justify treating Selective Conversion Failure as a
universal named mechanism.

---

# Falsification Requirements

Selective Conversion Failure and the candidate conversion framework should be
revised or rejected if future evidence demonstrates that:

1. materially relevant knowledge was not actually possessed by the
   organization before failure;
2. information reached the correct authority with adequate context and
   significance;
3. the authority recognized the risk;
4. corrective action was appropriately authorized and fully implemented;
5. effectiveness was verified;
6. the organization learned from repeated abnormal outcomes;
7. and catastrophic failure still occurred primarily through a materially
   different mechanism.

The framework must also be revised if applied diagnostic use shows that the
proposed conversion boundaries cannot be distinguished reliably or do not
produce actionable organizational insight.
