# Candidate Review

## F1 — Relevant Technical Knowledge Existed Before 51-L

**Eligibility**

**ELIGIBLE WITH QUALIFICATION**

**Exact eligible wording**

Before mission 51-L, NASA and Morton Thiokol possessed substantial documented
technical knowledge concerning Solid Rocket Motor field-joint behavior,
O-ring erosion and blow-by, joint rotation, criticality, temperature-related
concerns, prior flight anomalies, and potentially catastrophic seal failure.

This knowledge was distributed unevenly, and the record does not establish
that every participant possessed or understood every part of it.

**Primary evidence**

- CH6-E02
- CH6-E03
- CH6-E05
- CH6-E06
- CH6-E08
- CH6-E09
- CH6-E12
- CH6-E14
- CH5-E05
- CH5-E07
- CH4-E14

**Mandatory qualification**

- CH5-Q01
- CH6-Q01
- CH6-Q02
- CH6-Q03
- CH6-Q05

**Prohibited overstatement**

This finding must not claim:

- universal individual knowledge;
- complete pre-51-L understanding of the failure mechanism;
- complete agreement regarding temperature effects;
- certainty that the exact Challenger failure sequence was foreseeable.

## F2 — Institutional Possession Did Not Produce Common Decision-Level Awareness

**Eligibility**

**ELIGIBLE WITH QUALIFICATION**

**Exact eligible wording**

Although relevant O-ring and field-joint knowledge existed within NASA and
Morton Thiokol, material portions of that history, anomaly record, engineering
concern, and risk information did not reach, or were not represented
consistently before, all higher-level officials involved in readiness and
launch decisions for 51-L.

**Primary evidence**

- CH5-E01
- CH5-E02

- CH6-E14

- CH7-E10
- CH7-E15
- CH7-E16

- CH2-E18

**Mandatory qualification**

Message reception and interpretation were not uniform.

## F10 — Selective Conversion Failure

**Eligibility**

**HOLD**

"Selective Conversion Failure" remains a synthesis-level explanatory construct.

It is not yet an eligible Challenger finding.

**Reason for hold**

The phrase:

- is Institute-generated;
- is not terminology established by the Rogers Commission;
- combines multiple separately traceable organizational conditions;
- implies a unifying causal mechanism not yet independently validated;
- creates premature generalization risk.

The underlying evidence should first enter the case study as bounded findings.

Only afterward may the synthesis construct be reconsidered.

**Prohibited advancement**

Do not promote Selective Conversion Failure into:

- the Challenger findings section;
- Institutional Memory terminology;
- framework;
- axiom;
- theorem;
- Canon.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Q4 — Do F1–F6 establish a causal sequence?

**Decision**

Not yet.

They establish a set of interacting organizational conditions.

The evidence supports relationships among:

- knowledge existence;
- uneven distribution;
- inconsistent severity representation;
- incomplete escalation;
- ineffective safety redundancy;
- continued acceptance of anomaly history.

The current record does not justify treating that list as a strict deterministic
causal sequence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Q5 — Must findings be domain-bounded?

**Decision**

Yes.

Chapter IX and other qualifying evidence demonstrate that NASA could identify,
process, and act on hazards in other domains.

Organizational findings should therefore be bounded to:

- the Shuttle program where appropriate;
- the O-ring problem where appropriate;
- the 51-L decision system where appropriate.

They must not be framed as universal NASA characteristics.
