# D1 — Cross-Case Research Priority

The established Challenger findings should be tested in this order:

1. **CF-02 — Higher-level decision channels received incomplete representations
   of existing knowledge**
2. **CF-03 — O-ring risk significance was represented inconsistently**
3. **CF-04 — Escalation did not reliably place material risk information before
   higher-level authority**
4. **CF-05 — Independent safety architecture did not provide effective
   redundancy**
5. **CF-06 — Repeated successful operation coexisted with increasing acceptance
   of documented anomaly**
6. **CF-08 — The same institution successfully recognized and acted on hazards
   elsewhere**
7. **CF-01 — Relevant technical knowledge existed before failure**
8. **CF-07 — Schedule and resource pressure formed a constrained operating
   environment**

This ranking reflects **cross-case explanatory value**, not importance within
the Challenger history.

# D2 — Findings Less Likely to Become Standalone General Constructs

Three findings appear especially important as controls or foundations rather
than likely standalone Institutional Memory constructs.

## CF-01

CF-01 establishes the historical prerequisite that relevant knowledge existed.

It is essential evidence, but "knowledge existed before failure" alone is not a
sufficient theory of institutional memory.

## CF-07

CF-07 is explicitly contextual.

It is important for understanding institutional operating conditions, but the
Challenger record does not justify treating schedule or resource pressure as a
general causal mechanism.

## CF-08

CF-08 is comparative counterevidence.

Its primary theoretical value is to prevent an unsupported claim of universal
organizational incapacity.

# D3 — Highest-Priority Synthesis Proposition

## Decision

**YES — CF-09 is the highest-priority synthesis proposition for cross-case
testing.**

CF-09 asks whether:

> institutional possession of knowledge

and

> decision availability of knowledge

are empirically distinguishable states.

The Challenger record strongly supports that distinction.

It remains withheld from established findings because its formulation combines
multiple empirical findings and carries framework-development implications.

The proposition is nevertheless highly testable.

A second case can independently determine:

1. whether the organization possessed relevant knowledge;
2. whether the knowledge was preserved;
3. whether it was retrievable;
4. whether it reached relevant actors;
5. whether its severity was represented accurately;
6. whether it reached authority;
7. whether it possessed sufficient procedural or decision force to affect
   action.

If the distinction does not recur, CF-09 should not advance.

# D4 — Selective Conversion Failure

## Decision

**HOLD — leading integrative hypothesis**

Selective Conversion Failure remains the strongest integrative explanation
generated from the Challenger synthesis.

It is not validated.

It must not be protected from falsification.

The hypothesis currently describes a possible pattern in which:

- relevant knowledge exists;
- knowledge remains institutionally preserved;
- distribution is incomplete;
- significance changes or fragments;
- escalation is incomplete;
- independent control functions fail;
- yet the organization demonstrates effective risk response elsewhere.

A second case must test those components independently.

### Evidence that would weaken or falsify the hypothesis

Selective Conversion Failure would be weakened if a high-quality second case
shows that:

- relevant knowledge genuinely did not exist before failure;
- relevant knowledge existed and was complete at the decision point;
- severity was represented accurately;
- escalation functioned correctly;
- independent oversight had the information and authority required;
- decisionmakers possessed the relevant information in usable form;
- and failure nevertheless occurred primarily through another mechanism.

The hypothesis should also be weakened if its proposed components fail to recur
together or if a simpler competing explanation accounts for the evidence more
completely.

# D6 — Required Characteristics of the Second Case

The second case must maximize **falsification value**.

Required characteristics:

1. **Primary-source depth**

   A substantial authoritative investigative record must exist.

2. **Pre-event knowledge reconstruction**

   The record must permit reconstruction of what the institution knew before
   failure.

3. **Organizational traceability**

   Organizational units, management levels, reports, reviews, communication
   paths, and decision authorities must be observable.

4. **Independent failure anchor**

   The technical or operational failure mechanism must be independently
   established.

5. **Contradictory evidence**

   The record must contain evidence capable of weakening the Institutional
   Memory hypothesis.

6. **Time depth**

   The case should include a meaningful prehistory of anomalies, corrective
   action, review, or changing operational practice.

7. **Identifiable decision point**

   There must be a point or process where institutional knowledge plausibly
   could have affected action.

8. **Comparative distance from Challenger**

   The institution should differ enough from NASA to reduce organizational
   overfitting.

9. **Within-institution control possibility**

   Ideally, the same organization should show examples of hazards it handled
   successfully.

10. **Source preservability**

    Primary sources must be capable of preservation in the research repository
    with provenance, hashes, and stable citation.

# D7 — Preferred Case Domain

## Decision

**Nuclear / industrial safety**

This domain currently provides the strongest research design for the second
case.

### Advantages

- extensive authoritative accident investigations;
- explicit engineering and operational safety barriers;
- formal risk and hazard-management systems;
- independent regulatory and assurance functions;
- long operational histories;
- observable inspection, maintenance, and corrective-action systems;
- identifiable decision structures;
- substantial pre-event documentation;
- high potential for contradictory evidence.

### Comparative advantage

Nuclear / industrial safety is structurally different enough from the Shuttle
program to reduce the risk that a repeated pattern merely reflects NASA,
aerospace engineering, or Shuttle-specific governance.

### Boundary

No exact second case is selected by this review.

Specific cases must be scored competitively in the next research pass.

# D8 — Evidence Against the Emerging Institutional Memory Hypothesis

Future research must actively search for evidence against the Challenger-derived
hypothesis.

Material counterevidence would include a case where:

1. the relevant knowledge genuinely did not exist before failure;
2. the organization possessed the knowledge and decisionmakers also possessed
   it completely;
3. severity and uncertainty were represented accurately at every relevant
   organizational level;
4. escalation to authority worked as designed;
5. independent safety or assurance functions received the information and
   exercised appropriate authority;
6. anomaly history did not become more acceptable after repeated successful
   operation;
7. the failure occurred because of an unforeseeable technical mechanism rather
   than organizational knowledge handling;
8. institutional pressure was absent or demonstrably irrelevant;
9. the organization failed despite an effective knowledge and decision system;
10. another simpler explanatory model explains the event better.

Such evidence must not be treated as an inconvenience.

It is necessary to determine whether the emerging hypothesis is valid.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# D9 — Conditions for Retiring or Revising Selective Conversion Failure

Selective Conversion Failure should be substantially revised or retired if:

1. multiple well-documented cases fail to reproduce its core components;
2. institutional knowledge is repeatedly shown to reach authority accurately
   without preventing comparable failures;
3. independent oversight repeatedly functions effectively in comparable
   failures;
4. severity representation proves unrelated to decision outcomes;
5. anomaly acceptance does not recur;
6. the proposed components behave independently rather than as a coherent
   mechanism;
7. the hypothesis requires increasingly broad exceptions to survive;
8. another explanation accounts for more evidence with fewer assumptions.

The Institute must prefer a simpler or better-supported explanation if one
emerges.

# Cross-Case Research Questions

The Challenger case establishes eight research directions.

## RQ-01 — Knowledge Existence Versus Decision Use

Does relevant knowledge exist within an institution before failure while
remaining incomplete, unavailable, or operationally ineffective at the point
of decision?

## RQ-02 — Distribution, Representation, and Escalation

Are these empirically distinct organizational states:

- information distribution;
- severity representation;
- escalation to authority?

## RQ-03 — Independent Safety Redundancy

Under what conditions does independent safety or assurance successfully
intervene when operational management does not adequately process known risk?

## RQ-04 — Successful Operation and Accepted Anomaly

Does repeated successful operation in the presence of known anomaly alter the
organization's operational treatment of the anomaly?

## RQ-05 — Environmental Pressure Versus Decision Coercion

How should institutional workload and capacity pressure be distinguished from
direct coercion of a specific decision?

## RQ-06 — Selective Organizational Performance

Why can the same institution respond effectively to some hazards and
ineffectively to another?

## RQ-07 — Institutional Possession Versus Decision Availability

Does another case independently demonstrate that organizational possession of
knowledge and decision availability are meaningfully different states?

## RQ-08 — Selective Conversion Failure

Does another independent case reproduce the combination currently hypothesized
from Challenger?
