## Purpose

This synthesis tests competing explanations for the organizational conditions
surrounding the Challenger accident against the complete selected Rogers
Commission evidence set.

The synthesis follows the Institute research sequence:

**primary source → evidence → synthesis → findings → framework → Canon**

This document occupies the **synthesis** stage only.

Evidence outranks interpretation.

# Competing-Hypothesis Evaluation

## H1 — Knowledge Absence

**Hypothesis**

The organization failed because the relevant technical knowledge did not exist
before the 51-L decision.

**Supporting evidence**

Limited.

Technical uncertainty existed, particularly during earlier periods of Shuttle
development.

Relevant qualifying records include:

- CH5-Q01
- CH6-Q01
- CH6-Q02
- CH6-Q03

**Contradictory evidence**

Strong and longitudinal:

- CH6-E02
- CH6-E03
- CH6-E05
- CH6-E06
- CH6-E08
- CH6-E09
- CH6-E12
- CH6-E14
- CH5-E05
- CH5-E07
- CH4-E14

**Cross-chapter assessment**

Chapter VI documents technical knowledge extending back years before 51-L.
Chapter V establishes that specific low-temperature concern existed immediately
before launch. Chapter IV independently establishes the technical relevance of
temperature and seal behavior.

**What is established**

Relevant knowledge existed before the final launch decision.

**What is not established**

Every participant possessed the same knowledge or understood it with equal
certainty.

**Evidence strength**

Strongly contradicted as a primary explanation.

**Candidate status**

**REJECT**

# Cross-Chapter Conflict Resolution — Pass 1

## Conflict 1 — Chapter II versus Chapter V

Chapter II states that no critical temperature issue was identified to
management before countdown and fueling continued.

Chapter V documents an explicit Thiokol low-temperature concern, an initial
recommendation against launch below prior experience, and continued engineering
opposition.

These statements should not be treated as mutually exclusive.

The record demonstrates that concern existed within the decision process while
also demonstrating that it did not survive into every management representation
as a critical issue.

**Status:** Strong evidence for further investigation of transmission,
interpretation, and management-level differences.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conflict 2 — Institutional possession versus decisionmaker possession

Chapter VI demonstrates that NASA and Thiokol possessed extensive O-ring
knowledge.

Chapter V demonstrates that final launch decisionmakers lacked important
portions of that knowledge.

The evidence therefore requires a distinction between:

- institutional possession;
- local possession;
- storage;
- distribution;
- retrieval;
- awareness;
- interpretation;
- decision availability.

**Status:** Conflict substantially resolved conceptually; causal significance
still requires findings review.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conflict 3 — Institutional pressure versus direct coercion

Chapter VIII establishes substantial schedule, resource, workload, and customer
pressure.

The same chapter states that no evidence showed outside intervention in the
specific 51-L launch decision.

**Status:** Distinction established.

Institutional pressure environment and direct decision coercion are separate
propositions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conflict 4 — Failed safety system versus functioning learning elsewhere

Chapter VII documents serious failures in Shuttle safety architecture.

Chapter IX documents successful safety intervention and organizational learning
in other domains.

**Status:** Generalized NASA learning failure rejected.

The evidence instead requires a domain-sensitive explanation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conflict 5 — Risk acceptance versus technical uncertainty

Both are supported.

Management possessed genuine technical rationales and uncertainty remained,
while the organization simultaneously tolerated increasing anomaly history,
repeated waivers, inconsistent criticality, and accepted-risk treatment.

**Status:** Neither explanation eliminates the other.

Technical uncertainty is a material qualifier to, not a replacement for, the
organizational evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conflict 6 — NASA delayed for some hazards but not the O-ring hazard

NASA repeatedly delayed 51-L because of recognized weather, wind, hardware,
and ice conditions.

The O-ring concern did not achieve equivalent launch-stopping status.

**Status:** Unresolved at the findings level.

This is one of the most important surviving questions for Institutional Memory
analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conflict 7 — Successful flight versus safety margin

Repeated successful Shuttle flights occurred despite O-ring degradation.

Chapter IX separately states that prior successful operation cannot establish
adequate safety margin.

**Status:** Strong candidate evidence that operational success and actual safety
margin became organizationally distinguishable concepts.

Do not yet label this as a general Institute principle.

# Pass 1 Synthesis Result

The following primary explanations are rejected:

- H1 — Knowledge Absence
- H2 — Knowledge Loss
- H10 — Individual Decision Failure as the primary explanation
- H11 — Generalized NASA Learning Failure

The following remain material contributing explanations:

- H8 — Schedule / Resource Pressure
- H9 — Technical Uncertainty

The strongest surviving organizational hypotheses are:

1. H3 — Knowledge Distribution Failure
2. H4 — Meaning / Severity Degradation
3. H5 — Escalation / Authority Failure
4. H6 — Safety-Architecture Failure
5. H7 — Risk Normalization
6. H12 — Selective Conversion Failure

H12 currently provides the most complete integrative explanation because it
accounts for both:

- failure of the O-ring knowledge system; and
- evidence that NASA successfully learned and acted in other safety domains.

This does **not** establish H12 as a case-study finding.

# Additional Evidence Required Before Findings

Before advancing mature Challenger findings, the research should determine
whether the Rogers Commission record alone is sufficient for an initial
case-study finding set or whether independent primary/secondary corroboration is
required.

Particular attention should be given to:

1. independent corroboration of the January 27 Thiokol decision process;
2. organizational theory concerning normalization of deviance;
3. independent historical analysis of NASA safety culture;
4. later comparison with Columbia;
5. evidence distinguishing information storage from organizational use;
6. evidence concerning how severity, ownership, and authority affect knowledge
   conversion.
