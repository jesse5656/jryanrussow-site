# BP Texas City Findings Establishment Review

## Review Identity

**Research program:** Institutional Memory  
**Case:** BP Texas City Refinery Explosion  
**Primary case source:** BP-SRC-002  
**Atomic evidence:** 585 observations  
**Qualifying / contradictory evidence:** 89 records  
**Source status:** EVIDENCE-COMPLETE  

---

# Purpose

This review reconciles the complete BP Texas City evidence corpus and
determines which case-level propositions are sufficiently supported to become
established BP findings.

The objective is not to reproduce every CSB conclusion as an Institute
finding.

The objective is to preserve the smallest defensible set of findings that
captures the case's material organizational and institutional-memory
architecture without prematurely converting the case into cross-case theory.

---

# Establishment Standard

A BP finding may be established only when:

1. it is materially supported by the atomic ledger;
2. material qualifiers and contrary evidence do not defeat it;
3. it does not depend exclusively on a post-event recommendation;
4. retrospective CSB conclusions remain identified as retrospective evidence;
5. organizational levels are not silently collapsed;
6. it is not merely a restatement of another proposed BP finding; and
7. it remains case-specific.

Cross-case findings, framework propositions, and Canon propositions remain
prohibited.

---

# Reconciliation Conclusions

## R1 — Knowledge Existence Versus Effective Organizational Conversion

The BP corpus contains extensive evidence that serious process-safety hazards,
equipment deficiencies, staffing concerns, prior incidents, audit findings,
safety-culture problems, and organizational weaknesses were known before
March 23, 2005.

The record does not support a simple "BP did not know" explanation.

The more defensible case question is whether known information was reliably
converted into:

- decision-level awareness;
- corrective action;
- equipment repair;
- engineering redesign;
- training;
- procedural change;
- resource allocation;
- organizational learning; and
- sustained risk control.

---

## R2 — Formal Systems Versus Effective Implementation

The case contains formal systems for:

- incident reporting;
- Process Safety Management;
- Management of Change;
- training;
- mechanical integrity;
- audits;
- performance measurement;
- work orders; and
- safety governance.

The evidence therefore does not support a claim that BP operated without
formal safety systems.

The recurrent deficiency was ineffective implementation, incomplete closure,
misapplication, poor information flow, weak follow-through, or inadequate
measurement.

---

## R3 — Root Causes Versus Institute Findings

CSB Section 12 provides retrospective root and contributing causes.

Those causal classifications are powerful evidence but are not automatically
identical to Russow Institute case findings.

The Institute findings below are case-level propositions derived from the
complete evidence architecture.

# Findings Establishment Decisions

## BF-01 — Material Process-Safety Knowledge Existed Before the Explosion

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

Before March 23, 2005, BP Texas City and BP organizational systems possessed
substantial documented knowledge of process-safety hazards relevant to the
event, including recurring blowdown releases, abnormal splitter startups,
instrument problems, mechanical-integrity deficiencies, staffing concerns,
training deficiencies, organizational instability, safety-culture problems,
and weaknesses identified through audits and assessments.

Institutional possession was distributed and uneven; the finding does not
assert that every individual possessed every relevant fact.

**Primary evidence domains**

- prior incidents and near misses;
- audits and assessments;
- mechanical-integrity records;
- staffing studies;
- training records;
- Telos and internal safety reviews;
- Section 12 causal synthesis.

**Establishment class**

DIRECT HISTORICAL / ORGANIZATIONAL KNOWLEDGE

---

## BF-02 — Known Safety Information Did Not Reliably Produce Corrective Action

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

Across multiple systems, recognized hazards and identified deficiencies did
not reliably produce timely, completed, and effective corrective action.

The corpus documents unresolved investigation actions, incomplete PSM action
items, uncorrected instrumentation problems, repeated blowdown events,
deferred maintenance, incomplete training, and known hazards that remained in
service.

This finding does not imply that BP took no corrective action; the corpus also
contains genuine improvement initiatives and management responses.

**Primary evidence anchors**

- BP-E-557;
- BP-E-559;
- BP-E-560;
- BP-E-562;
- BP-E-564;
- BP-E-579;
- BP-E-582;
- Section 9 warning/response evidence;
- Section 12 causal synthesis.

**Establishment class**

ORGANIZATIONAL PROCESS

## BF-03 — Incident Reporting and Learning Did Not Preserve a Complete Operational Risk History

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

BP Texas City's reporting and learning architecture did not reliably capture,
integrate, investigate, and retain the significance of recurring process-safety
events.

The CSB reconstructed significant prior blowdown history from fragmented
sources because no single reporting system contained the complete record.
Several serious incidents were recorded only as environmental events, some
were not entered into an incident database, and only a minority received
safety investigations.

The finding concerns institutional preservation and learning, not merely
database technology.

**Primary evidence anchors**

- BP-E-556;
- BP-E-558;
- BP-E-561;
- BP-E-562;
- BP-E-564;
- BP-E-565;
- BP-Q-083;
- BP-Q-084.

**Establishment class**

ORGANIZATIONAL PROCESS / INSTITUTIONAL MEMORY

## BF-04 — Personal-Safety Performance Did Not Provide an Adequate View of Process-Safety Risk

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

BP Texas City achieved improving personal-injury performance while serious
process-safety conditions persisted and, in important respects, deteriorated.

Management attention, performance measures, campaigns, and rewards placed
substantial emphasis on personal safety.

The evidence shows that low injury rates were not an adequate proxy for the
health of process-safety systems or catastrophic-risk controls.

**Primary evidence domains**

- Section 9 performance-measurement evidence;
- Section 10 safety-culture analysis;
- Section 12 root-cause synthesis;
- BP-E-554.

**Establishment class**

ORGANIZATIONAL MEASUREMENT / RISK REPRESENTATION

## BF-05 — Mechanical-Integrity Systems Did Not Reliably Restore Known Safety-Critical Equipment

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

Known problems with safety-critical instruments and equipment persisted into
the March 23 startup.

The evidence includes stale or incorrect calibration information, incomplete
repair, closed work orders despite unfinished repairs, ineffective functional
testing, nonoperational equipment, and known instrument impairment before
startup.

The finding does not assert that every instrument failure had the same level
of causal certainty.

## BF-06 — Hazardous Startup Was Not Supported by Sufficiently Reliable Training, Staffing, and Technical Assistance

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

The startup occurred within a system in which training, staffing, fatigue
control, role documentation, and technically experienced assistance were not
sufficiently reliable for a hazardous abnormal operating state.

The record includes earlier staffing warnings, unresolved staffing concerns,
incomplete and stale training, limited abnormal-situation preparation,
extended work schedules, and competing duties affecting experienced startup
support.

This finding does not reduce the event to individual operator error and does
not claim that operators received no training.

**Primary evidence anchors**

- BP-E-533 through BP-E-540;
- BP-E-541 through BP-E-549;
- BP-E-553;
- BP-Q-078;
- BP-Q-080;
- BP-Q-081.

## BF-07 — Management of Change Did Not Reliably Control Technical and Organizational Change

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

BP Texas City possessed a formal Management-of-Change system, but the evidence
shows repeated failures to invoke, apply, review, or close MOC appropriately
for equipment, procedures, staffing, organizational arrangements, and
turnaround siting.

The finding therefore concerns implementation and scope, not the absence of an
MOC policy.

**Primary evidence anchors**

- BP-E-507;
- BP-E-508;
- BP-E-509;
- BP-E-584;
- BP-E-585;
- BP-Q-088;
- Section 10 organizational-change evidence;
- Section 12 causal synthesis.

**Establishment class**

ORGANIZATIONAL PROCESS / CHANGE CONTROL

## BF-08 — Resource, Production, and Organizational Pressures Reduced Process-Safety Capacity

**Decision:** ESTABLISH AS CONTEXTUAL CAUSAL CONDITION

The evidence supports that budget pressure, maintenance spending constraints,
staffing pressure, production demands, leadership turnover, and repeated
organizational restructuring reduced available process-safety capacity at
Texas City.

The record also shows instances of management resistance to cuts and genuine
safety-improvement efforts.

This finding therefore does not claim that cost cutting alone caused the
explosion.

**Primary evidence domains**

- Section 9 warning and resource evidence;
- Telos assessment;
- Section 10 organizational-change analysis;
- staffing evidence;
- Section 12 causal synthesis.

**Establishment class**

CONTEXTUAL / ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY

---

## BF-09 — Leadership and Oversight Responses Were Insufficient Relative to the Warning Record

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

Before the explosion, BP site management, senior refining leadership, and
corporate oversight systems received repeated indications of significant
process-safety weaknesses.

The evidence shows genuine responses and improvement initiatives, but the
aggregate response did not correct the major underlying process-safety
deficiencies before March 23, 2005.

This finding preserves distinctions among BP Board oversight, senior executive
management, and Texas City site management rather than treating "management"
as one actor.

**Primary evidence domains**

- audits and assessments;
- Safety Reality;
- Telos;
- GHSER;
- resource and investment decisions;
- Section 10 corporate leadership;
- BP-E-460 through BP-E-472.

**Establishment class**

ORGANIZATIONAL OVERSIGHT

---

## BF-10 — Repeated Abnormal Outcomes Became Insufficient Triggers for System Reassessment

**Decision:** ESTABLISH

Recurring abnormal startups, relief events, vapor releases, equipment
malfunctions, and prior incidents did not consistently trigger reassessment of
the underlying system design, operating assumptions, instrumentation,
procedures, or risk controls.

Successful continuation of operations coexisted with repeated abnormal
conditions.

The finding does not assert that every abnormal event was identical to the
March 23 event.

**Primary evidence domains**

- historical startup records;
- Appendix Q prior incidents;
- BP-E-555 through BP-E-567;
- process-design history;
- incident investigation evidence;
- Section 12 synthesis.

**Establishment class**

ORGANIZATIONAL LEARNING / NORMALIZATION OF DEVIATION

# Material Not Established as Separate Findings

## OSHA Enforcement

The CSB identified significant limitations in pre-event OSHA PSM enforcement.

This is important contextual and regulatory evidence.

It is not established here as a core BP organizational finding because the
research program's current case question concerns how BP acquired, preserved,
used, and failed to convert organizational knowledge.

Hold for later cross-case or contextual analysis.

---

## Specific Ignition Source

Appendix H leaves the north-end diesel truck as the likely ignition candidate
while explicitly stating that modeling alone could not confirm it.

The ignition source therefore does not become an Institute finding beyond the
qualified technical evidence already in the ledger.

---

## Specific Release Quantity

Modeled release quantities remain engineering estimates rather than direct
measurements.

They are not promoted into a case-level finding.

---

## Recommendations

Post-event CSB recommendations remain recommendations.

They do not become findings merely because they identify desirable controls.

---

# Cross-Cutting Qualifications

1. BP possessed formal safety-management systems.
2. BP undertook genuine safety-improvement initiatives.
3. Not every warning was available to every organizational actor.
4. Retrospective CSB causal synthesis is distinguished from contemporaneous
   organizational knowledge.
5. Instrument-failure certainty varies by instrument.
6. Fatigue is a likely contributing condition rather than a deterministic
   explanation.
7. Cost/resource pressure is not established as a sole cause.
8. Operator training existed; the deficiency concerns adequacy, currency,
   completion, specificity, and abnormal-situation preparation.
9. Board, senior-executive, site-management, supervisory, operator, contractor,
   and regulator roles must remain separate.
10. BP findings remain case-specific.
