# Review Standard

An appendix requires further atomic extraction when it contains evidence that
is both:

1. materially relevant to causation, qualification, organizational knowledge,
   technical mechanism, human factors, or exclusion of a plausible cause; and

2. not already represented with adequate specificity in
   `009-BP-TEXAS-CITY-CSB-FINAL-REPORT.md`.

Detailed calculations, repeated chronology, photographs, duplicated tables,
or additional numerical precision do not automatically require a new atomic
record when the material proposition is already captured.

Counterevidence and explicit exclusion of plausible causes receive the same
preservation priority as inculpatory evidence.

# High-Priority Material Evidence

## Appendix L — Staffing Concerns

Appendix L contains contemporaneous pre-event staffing evidence, including:

- a 1996 assessment concluding that existing staffing arrangements would not
  permit safe handling of unit upsets;
- a 2000 union warning concerning operation below safe-off staffing levels;
- concern that reduced extra-board staffing generated excessive overtime and
  fatigue;
- an unresolved staffing grievance; and
- a BP lesson from the Lavera refinery that sufficient qualified staff with
  diagnostic skills must be available during startups.

This material requires comparison against existing staffing records.

## Appendix O — Operator Training

Appendix O contains detailed evidence concerning:

- Basic Operator Training;
- generic troubleshooting instruction;
- on-the-job learning limitations;
- computer-based training design;
- training certification;
- incomplete required training;
- board-operator SOIs;
- individual performance-development plans; and
- abnormal-situation preparation.

Of particular significance, company records showed that no board-certified
individual had completed more than 44 percent of required training.

## Appendix P — Distraction Not a Contributing Factor

Appendix P contains material counterevidence.

The CSB evaluated the safety meeting and telephone calls as possible
distractions and concluded that they did not likely contribute to the
incident.

The appendix separately identifies a narrower possible distraction outside
the control room: the Lead Operator was occupied with multiple other duties
and therefore was not available to assist the Board Operator during startup.

Both the cause exclusion and the narrower qualification must be preserved.

## Appendix T — BP Management-of-Change Policy

Appendix T contains detailed evidence concerning actual MOC-policy
misapplication involving:

- startup with a non-operable pressure-control valve;
- startup-procedure changes;
- blowdown-system changes;
- treatment of the 1997 blowdown replacement as replacement-in-kind;
- chaining open the sewer-discharge valve;
- degraded blowdown-drum internals;
- loss of service water; and
- trailer siting.

The appendix therefore provides important evidence distinguishing formal MOC
requirements from actual implementation.
