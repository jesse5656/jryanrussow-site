# 1. Purpose

This document defines the first bounded commercial pilot of the Institutional
Memory Diagnostic.

The purpose of the pilot is not merely to perform an organizational study.

The pilot must test whether The Russow Institute can convert the existing
Institutional Memory research and diagnostic methodology into a client
engagement that is:

- analytically defensible;
- operationally useful;
- understandable to executives;
- repeatable;
- economically viable;
- and worth approximately $10,000 to the client.

The pilot is simultaneously:

1. a real client engagement;
2. an applied test of the diagnostic methodology;
3. a test of delivery economics;
4. and a source of bounded field evidence for methodology improvement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Commercial Hypothesis

The commercial hypothesis is:

> An organization experiencing recurring operational, organizational, safety,
> quality, customer, execution, or corrective-action problems will pay
> approximately $10,000 for a rigorous diagnostic that identifies where
> consequential knowledge is failing to become effective organizational
> action and provides evidence-backed priorities for correction.

This hypothesis has not yet been commercially validated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Pilot Price

The initial pilot engagement price is:

**$10,000**

This is a fixed pilot price for a bounded Version 1 engagement.

The price is not intended to imply that all future Institutional Memory
Diagnostics will cost $10,000.

The Commercialization Thesis identifies a preliminary broader range of:

**$7,500–$25,000**

Future pricing must be informed by actual delivery effort, client value,
organizational complexity, and pilot results.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Ideal Pilot Client

The ideal pilot client is an established organization with:

- a real operating history;
- multiple employees or organizational roles;
- meaningful documentary evidence;
- identifiable management authority;
- at least one consequential recurring or unresolved organizational problem;
- leadership willing to permit evidence-based examination;
- and sufficient ability to act on resulting findings.

Strong pilot conditions may include:

- recurring customer complaints;
- repeated quality defects;
- repeated safety issues or near misses;
- recurring corrective actions;
- audit findings that reappear;
- operational problems that survive multiple attempts at correction;
- leadership transition;
- employee turnover affecting continuity;
- fragmented documentation;
- cross-department coordination problems;
- inconsistent execution;
- or a known gap between formal procedures and actual operating practice.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Poor Pilot Candidates

The first pilot should generally avoid organizations where:

- leadership only wants validation of a predetermined conclusion;
- material evidence will not be made available;
- relevant personnel cannot participate;
- the requested scope is enterprise-wide without meaningful boundaries;
- active litigation or privilege concerns make evidence access impractical;
- the client expects legal, accounting, engineering, cybersecurity, medical,
  regulatory, or other licensed professional conclusions outside Institute
  competence;
- the organization expects certification;
- the organization expects a numerical maturity score unsupported by the
  current methodology;
- or leadership has no realistic capacity or intention to act on findings.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Unit of Engagement

The $10,000 pilot is not an unrestricted examination of an entire enterprise.

The engagement must be bounded around:

- one principal organizational problem, risk, or recurring condition;

and normally:

- one operating site, business unit, department cluster, or comparable
  organizational boundary.

The Diagnostic may follow evidence across organizational boundaries when
necessary to understand the selected problem.

Expansion beyond the agreed boundary requires explicit scope review.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Initial Scope Boundary

The Version 1 pilot is designed around the following working limits:

## Principal Problem

One primary consequential organizational problem or recurring condition.

## Organizational Boundary

Normally one site, business unit, operating group, or bounded cross-functional
system.

## Evidence Period

Normally up to three years of relevant historical evidence.

Older evidence may be examined when necessary to establish recurrence,
institutional history, or causal context.

## Diagnostic Domains

All D1 through D12 remain analytically available.

Not every domain must produce a finding.

The absence of a finding in a domain must not be interpreted automatically as
proof of organizational strength.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Documentary Evidence Boundary

The initial pilot should target a manageable evidence set.

Working Version 1 boundary:

**Up to approximately 75 substantive client-provided documents or equivalent
records during primary intake.**

Examples include:

- procedures;
- policies;
- incident reports;
- corrective-action records;
- audit reports;
- inspection records;
- customer complaints;
- training records;
- meeting records;
- management communications;
- project records;
- quality records;
- maintenance records;
- operational metrics;
- organizational charts;
- change-management records;
- and related evidence.

Large databases, message archives, bulk exports, or thousands of individual
records are not automatically equivalent to one document.

If evidence volume materially exceeds the intended analytical effort, scope
must be reviewed before additional analysis is authorized.

The 75-document boundary is a pilot operating assumption, not a validated
universal threshold.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Interview Boundary

The Version 1 pilot should normally include:

**Up to 8 individual interviews.**

Target interview duration:

**Approximately 45–60 minutes each.**

Interview roles should be selected according to the selected organizational
problem and may include:

- executive sponsor;
- responsible manager;
- frontline employee;
- supervisor;
- quality representative;
- safety representative;
- operations representative;
- maintenance representative;
- project or process owner;
- or another person possessing material knowledge.

Participant selection should seek multiple organizational levels where
relevant.

The Diagnostic should not rely solely on management interviews.

Additional interviews require scope review.

The eight-interview boundary is a pilot operating assumption subject to
validation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Client Responsibilities

The client is responsible for:

- identifying an executive sponsor;
- defining the initial problem with the Institute;
- providing requested evidence;
- identifying relevant personnel;
- permitting interviews within the agreed scope;
- identifying confidentiality or access restrictions;
- responding to reasonable clarification requests;
- reviewing factual representations for material error;
- and participating in the final executive presentation.

The client is not entitled to dictate analytical findings.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Institute Responsibilities

The Russow Institute is responsible for:

- maintaining evidence provenance;
- distinguishing evidence from inference;
- preserving contradictory evidence;
- applying the D1–D12 instrument consistently;
- seeking successful conversion as counterevidence;
- avoiding unsupported causal claims;
- protecting organizational-level attribution;
- identifying limitations;
- developing traceable findings;
- performing internal adversarial review;
- and delivering findings in a form usable by organizational leadership.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Engagement Workflow

The candidate Version 1 workflow is:

## Phase 1 — Qualification

Determine whether the organization and proposed problem are suitable for the
Diagnostic.

## Phase 2 — Scope Definition

Define:

- principal problem;
- organizational boundary;
- evidence period;
- executive sponsor;
- anticipated evidence sources;
- interview population;
- and exclusions.

## Phase 3 — Evidence Request

Issue a bounded evidence request.

## Phase 4 — Evidence Intake

Receive, inventory, organize, and preserve provenance for supplied evidence.

## Phase 5 — Preliminary Review

Identify:

- material knowledge items;
- chronology;
- recurring conditions;
- apparent organizational boundaries;
- missing evidence;
- and interview requirements.

## Phase 6 — Interviews

Conduct targeted interviews using the Diagnostic Instrument.

## Phase 7 — Knowledge Trace Development

Reconstruct selected consequential knowledge items across relevant D1–D12
boundaries.

## Phase 8 — Domain Analysis

Apply evidence questions, analytical tests, and finding criteria.

## Phase 9 — Cross-Domain Synthesis

Identify interacting conversion failures without assuming a linear sequence.

## Phase 10 — Adversarial Review

Attempt to defeat, narrow, qualify, or reject material candidate findings.

## Phase 11 — Finding Development

Prepare evidence-backed findings and remediation priorities.

## Phase 12 — Executive Deliverable

Prepare the client report and leadership presentation.

## Phase 13 — Executive Presentation

Present:

- what was examined;
- what the evidence supports;
- what remains uncertain;
- where conversion appears to fail;
- what risks result;
- and what interventions should be prioritized.

## Phase 14 — Pilot Learning Review

Separately evaluate the methodology and commercial delivery process.

# 15. Economic Guardrails

The pilot should determine whether the Diagnostic can be delivered within an
economically sustainable labor envelope.

Working commercial interpretation:

- **≤ 50 hours:** strong delivery economics;
- **51–70 hours:** acceptable pilot range;
- **71–90 hours:** methodology or scope requires improvement;
- **> 90 hours:** current $10,000 configuration is likely commercially
  unsustainable without major redesign, automation, scope reduction, or
  higher pricing.

These are management guardrails for pilot evaluation.

They are not client-facing diagnostic scores and are not validated universal
commercial thresholds.

# 19. Finding Boundary

The Diagnostic may report a material finding only when the available evidence
supports the stated organizational condition.

Findings must distinguish:

- observed condition;
- supporting evidence;
- contradictory evidence;
- Institute inference;
- organizational boundary;
- operational consequence;
- recommended intervention;
- and verification requirement.

The Diagnostic must not convert:

- suspicion into fact;
- correlation into causation;
- organizational possession into universal individual awareness;
- isolated failure into enterprise-wide incapacity;
- contextual pressure into sole causation;
- or hypothesis into established framework proposition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. Remediation Boundary

The pilot includes prioritized remediation recommendations.

It does not automatically include implementation of those recommendations.

Implementation support, operating-system redesign, management-system
architecture, software development, recurring assurance, or continuing
advisory work constitutes potential follow-on scope.

This boundary is commercially important.

The Diagnostic identifies and prioritizes problems.

Follow-on services may address them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Unless separately authorized, the pilot does not include:

- psychological assessment;

- software implementation;

- certification;
- or recurring assurance.

# 22. Client Value Test

At completion, leadership should be asked whether the Diagnostic:

1. accurately represented the organizational problem;
2. connected evidence that had previously remained fragmented;
3. identified meaningful knowledge-to-action failures;
4. revealed material issues or relationships not previously understood;
5. distinguished symptoms from organizational conversion failures;
6. produced actionable priorities;
7. improved leadership's understanding of organizational risk;
8. provided conclusions leadership considers defensible;
9. justified the time required from client personnel;
10. would have justified approximately $10,000 of expenditure.

These questions test client-perceived value.

They do not by themselves validate the underlying framework.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 23. Methodology Success Criteria

The pilot should be considered methodologically successful when the Institute
can demonstrate that it:

- obtained sufficient evidence;
- applied D1–D12 without forcing every domain to produce a failure;
- reconstructed meaningful knowledge traces;
- preserved evidence/inference separation;
- retained contradictory evidence;
- identified successful conversion as counterevidence;
- produced findings capable of surviving adversarial review;
- generated actionable remediation priorities;
- identified limitations;
- and learned where the instrument requires modification.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 24. Commercial Success Criteria

The pilot should be considered commercially promising when:

- a real client accepts approximately $10,000 pricing or equivalent economic
  value;
- delivery remains within a sustainable labor envelope;
- leadership considers the deliverable materially useful;
- the scope can be explained clearly before sale;
- client evidence requirements are manageable;
- the engagement can be completed on a predictable schedule;
- the executive deliverable can be produced repeatably;
- and credible follow-on demand emerges from identified remediation needs.

One successful pilot does not establish a validated market.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 25. Pilot Failure Criteria

The pilot should trigger material redesign when:

- evidence requirements are routinely impractical;
- D1–D12 cannot be distinguished sufficiently in application;
- interviews dominate the evidence without corroboration;
- findings depend heavily on analyst intuition;
- contradictory evidence cannot be handled consistently;
- clients cannot understand the product;
- the deliverable does not create actionable value;
- labor materially exceeds the commercial envelope;
- scope cannot be controlled;
- or clients do not perceive approximately $10,000 of value.

Failure is useful evidence.

The purpose of the pilot is to discover whether the commercial and analytical
model survives contact with a real organization.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
