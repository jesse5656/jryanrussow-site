## 1. Purpose

Define, before the first pilot is executed, how The Russow Institute will
interpret the pilot's methodological, client-value, delivery, economic, and
commercial evidence.

The purpose is preregistration.

The Institute shall not wait until after the first engagement to decide what
would count as success, redesign evidence, falsification evidence, or a reason
to reopen research.

This framework does not create a new diagnostic domain.

It operationalizes the validation requirements already established by the
Diagnostic Specification, Pilot Engagement architecture, and Pilot
Authorization Package.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Core Commercial Question

The first pilot must help answer:

> Can The Russow Institute sell and competently perform an approximately
> $10,000 Institutional Memory Diagnostic using the intellectual property that
> exists today?

This question contains separate claims about:

1. saleability;
2. analytical competence;
3. client value;
4. scope control;
5. delivery feasibility;
6. economic viability;
7. repeatability;
8. and what should be learned before the next engagement.

No single metric answers all of them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Preregistration Rule

The validation criteria in this document shall be treated as the pre-pilot
baseline.

After pilot kickoff:

- material changes to validation criteria must be timestamped;
- the reason for the change must be recorded;
- the change must not be disguised as an original criterion;
- and post-hoc learning must be distinguished from preregistered evaluation.

The Institute may correct obvious clerical errors during execution.

It may not redefine success merely because the pilot produced an unexpected
result.

Unexpected or negative results are valid commercial and methodological
evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. What One Pilot Can and Cannot Establish

A single pilot may provide evidence about whether the current Diagnostic can:

- be sold to one real organization;
- be bounded operationally;
- obtain sufficient evidence;
- apply D1–D12 usefully;
- produce defensible conclusions;
- create leadership value;
- fit the intended labor and schedule envelope;
- and reveal requirements for controlled refinement.

A single pilot cannot by itself establish:

- universal validity of the candidate Institutional Memory framework;
- prevalence of Selective Conversion Failure;
- broad market demand;
- final target-client segmentation;
- final pricing;
- a universally repeatable delivery system;
- a mature sales narrative;
- or production technology requirements.

Those claims require additional evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Noncompensatory Validation Principle

The pilot shall not receive a composite numerical success score.

A strong result in one dimension may not silently compensate for failure in a
different blocking dimension.

Examples:

- client enthusiasm does not compensate for analytically unsupported findings;
- strong analytical findings do not compensate for commercially unsustainable
  labor;
- low labor does not compensate for lack of client value;
- a successful sale does not compensate for uncontrolled scope;
- and a useful engagement does not establish a universally validated framework.

Each material validation dimension shall therefore receive its own evidence
record and disposition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Required Validation Evidence Streams

The pilot learning review shall preserve evidence from at least these streams.

### 6.1 Qualification and Sales

Record:

- first substantive qualification date;
- commercial decision date;
- sales-cycle duration;
- organization role that sponsored or rejected the work;
- problem language used by the prospect;
- terms or concepts the prospect understood immediately;
- terms or concepts that caused confusion;
- objections;
- pricing reaction;
- scope reaction;
- confidentiality or evidence-access concerns;
- and reason for purchase, deferment, or refusal.

### 6.2 Scope and Delivery

Record:

- agreed principal problem;
- organizational boundary;
- evidence period;
- evidence count;
- interview count;
- client-caused delays;
- Institute-caused delays;
- requested scope changes;
- approved scope changes;
- rejected scope changes;
- rework;
- and elapsed delivery time.

### 6.3 Methodology

Record:

- D1–D12 domains used;
- domains producing findings;
- domains producing counterevidence;
- domains producing no supported conclusion;
- domain overlap or ambiguity;
- knowledge traces developed;
- candidate findings;
- narrowed findings;
- rejected findings;
- final findings;
- material contradictions;
- and analytical steps requiring unusual analyst judgment.

### 6.4 Client Value

Use the ten client-value questions already established in the Pilot Engagement
architecture and Pilot Measurement Record.

Preserve the client's substantive responses.

Do not reduce those responses to an invented satisfaction score.

### 6.5 Economics

Record all Institute labor by the existing activity categories.

Also record:

- quoted price;
- contracted price;
- collected revenue;
- direct travel expense;
- material third-party expense;
- unusual one-time pilot-development effort;
- and rework caused by methodology or scope defects.

### 6.6 Repeatability

For each material delivery step, classify what was observed as:

- **STANDARDIZABLE**
- **JUDGMENT-DEPENDENT**
- **CLIENT-SPECIFIC**
- **DEFECT / EXCEPTION**

Record why.

### 6.7 Research Feedback

Record:

- unsupported assumptions;
- successful organizational counterexamples;
- indistinguishable diagnostic boundaries;
- potentially missing mechanisms;
- falsifying or materially qualifying evidence;
- and defined research questions.

### 6.8 Technology Learning

Record technology-related friction only as evidence.

Examples include:

- provenance administration;
- evidence indexing;
- chronology reconstruction;
- cross-source linkage;
- contradiction tracking;
- repeated-pattern detection;
- interview-to-evidence linkage;
- finding traceability;
- and report production.

Technology observations do not authorize software development.

## 7. Validation Dimension A — Qualification and Product Clarity

### Supporting Evidence

Support exists when a real organizational sponsor can understand, without
material distortion:

- what the Diagnostic examines;
- what it does not examine;
- the one-problem / bounded-site model;
- the evidence and interview requirements;
- and the approximately-$10,000 commercial boundary.

### Warning Evidence

Record as a product-clarity defect when qualification repeatedly requires:

- reframing the service as ordinary compliance work;
- promising excluded professional services;
- implying a predetermined failure;
- excessive explanation before the buyer can understand the value;
- or materially changing the product to make it saleable.

One prospect's confusion does not establish market failure.

It does identify sales-language evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Validation Dimension B — Evidence Sufficiency

### Supporting Evidence

Evidence sufficiency is supported when the Institute can produce material
conclusions that are traceable to the bounded evidence set while:

- identifying material gaps;
- preserving provenance;
- distinguishing documentary evidence from interview testimony;
- retaining contradictory evidence;
- and avoiding unsupported completion of missing records.

The pilot need not use all 75 available records.

The approximately-75-record limit is a ceiling assumption, not a required
minimum.

### Redesign Evidence

Record a material evidence-design problem when:

- useful analysis routinely requires evidence far beyond the bounded intake;
- essential provenance cannot be maintained;
- the engagement depends predominantly on uncorroborated interviews;
- or meaningful conclusions require recreation of unavailable historical
  records.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Validation Dimension C — D1–D12 Analytical Discriminability

### Supporting Evidence

D1–D12 are supported for continued testing when they help the analyst
distinguish materially different organizational conditions without forcing a
failure into every domain.

A successful organization may produce:

- effective conversion;
- counterevidence;
- no material finding;
- or an unresolved state.

Those are valid outcomes.

### Material Redesign Evidence

Record a methodology defect when:

- multiple domains cannot be distinguished in practice;
- domain assignment is largely arbitrary;
- useful conclusions depend mainly on analyst intuition outside the
  instrument;
- or a material recurring mechanism cannot reasonably be represented within
  the current analytical structure.

Do not create a new domain during the pilot merely to make the framework appear
complete.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Validation Dimension D — Finding Defensibility

A final finding must:

- state an observed organizational condition;
- identify supporting evidence;
- preserve contradictory or qualifying evidence;
- remain within the supported organizational boundary;
- distinguish evidence from Institute inference;
- avoid unsupported causation;
- and survive the existing adversarial review process.

Methodological support requires that final client conclusions can survive this
process.

A pilot is not methodologically successful merely because it generates many
findings.

A defensible conclusion that existing controls function effectively may be a
methodologically useful pilot result.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Validation Dimension E — Client Value

Client value is supported when leadership indicates that the final work is:

- materially accurate;
- defensible;
- useful for decision-making;
- and actionable enough to justify the client time required.

The existing ten client-value questions shall be used.

Particular attention shall be paid to whether the Diagnostic:

- connected evidence that had remained fragmented;
- revealed a relationship leadership had not adequately connected;
- distinguished symptoms from conversion failures;
- and created practical remediation priorities.

### Price-Value Separation

Client value and price acceptance are related but separate.

A client may find the work useful while rejecting the price.

A client may also purchase work that ultimately fails to produce sufficient
value.

Both facts must be recorded independently.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Validation Dimension F — Delivery and Schedule

The current delivery target is approximately:

**3–4 weeks**

measured from substantially complete evidence availability and required
participant availability.

Record separately:

- Institute delivery time;
- client waiting time;
- evidence-access delay;
- interview-scheduling delay;
- and rework delay.

A client-caused delay shall not be silently counted as Institute execution
failure.

An Institute-caused delay shall not be silently attributed to the client.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Validation Dimension G — Labor Economics

Use the existing Pilot Engagement guardrails.

### Strong Delivery Economics

**50 hours or less**

### Acceptable Pilot Range

**51–70 hours**

### Methodology / Scope Improvement Required

**71–90 hours**

### Current Configuration Likely Unsustainable

**More than 90 hours**

These thresholds evaluate the current approximately-$10,000 pilot
configuration.

They do not establish universal future pricing.

All Institute labor must be counted.

One-time development effort may be identified separately but shall not be
silently excluded from the record.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Validation Dimension H — Scope Integrity

Scope control is supported when the engagement remains materially within:

- one principal problem;
- one site or equivalent bounded organizational system;
- approximately 75 substantive records;
- approximately 8 interviews;
- approximately 3–4 weeks;
- and the agreed professional-service exclusions.

Any expansion shall be recorded as:

- requested;
- accepted;
- rejected;
- or separately scoped.

A pilot that creates value only after becoming an unbounded consulting
engagement does not validate the bounded Diagnostic configuration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Price Evidence States

Record the strongest price evidence actually obtained.

### DIRECT PRICE EVIDENCE

A real client contracts for approximately $10,000 and the commercial obligation
is genuine.

Collected revenue shall be recorded separately from contracted revenue.

### PARTIAL PRICE EVIDENCE

The engagement is contracted at a materially discounted pilot price but the
client provides explicit evidence regarding willingness or unwillingness to pay
approximately $10,000 for the demonstrated value.

### INDIRECT PRICE EVIDENCE

The organization discusses price or value but no sufficiently reliable
commercial commitment is obtained.

### NO PRICE EVIDENCE

The pilot structure does not meaningfully test willingness to pay.

A free or heavily subsidized engagement shall not be represented as validation
of approximately-$10,000 willingness to pay.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. Sales-Narrative Evidence Capture

The pilot and qualification process shall capture evidence for a later sales
narrative without finalizing that narrative in advance.

Record:

- the prospect's own description of the problem;
- which Institute terms were immediately understood;
- which terms required explanation;
- the most compelling value proposition expressed by the prospect;
- objections;
- reasons for urgency or lack of urgency;
- perceived alternatives;
- evidence-access concerns;
- sponsor / buyer role;
- and pricing reaction.

Do not convert one prospect's wording into a universal market message.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. Repeatability Test

For each pilot phase, ask:

1. Can the same step be performed using the existing artifact on the next
   engagement?
2. What required client-specific adaptation?
3. What depended on analyst judgment?
4. What generated avoidable rework?
5. What should become a standard operating rule?
6. What should remain discretionary professional judgment?

### Repeatability Support

Repeatability is supported when the core workflow can be reused with bounded
client-specific adaptation.

### Repeatability Defect

Record a material defect when delivery depends on substantial reinvention of
the method, forms, evidence structure, or analytical logic for the individual
client.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 18. Methodology Change Threshold

Post-pilot changes shall be classified.

### Minor Refinement

Examples:

- clearer wording;
- form layout improvement;
- question ordering;
- redundant prompt removal;
- workflow clarification.

These changes do not alter the underlying analytical model.

### Material Methodology Revision

Examples:

- redefining a D1–D12 boundary;
- changing evidence sufficiency requirements materially;
- changing finding criteria;
- materially changing adversarial review;
- changing the engagement unit or analytical boundary.

These require explicit governed human review and a versioned methodology
change.

### Architecture-Level Change

A change that alters the governing product architecture, authorization model,
or Institute operating architecture requires the applicable ACP or OCP process.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 19. Research Reopening Rule

Historical or framework research shall not reopen merely because the pilot
raises interesting questions.

Research may reopen only when the field evidence establishes a defined need,
including:

- a material diagnostic mechanism not represented adequately by the current
  framework;
- a successful organizational counterexample that materially challenges an
  analytical assumption;
- evidence that D1–D12 cannot distinguish conditions the Diagnostic must
  distinguish;
- falsifying or materially qualifying evidence relevant to an existing
  research claim;
- or a specific question that must be answered before the next controlled
  pilot can be performed competently.

The research question must be written before new research begins.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 20. Technology Requirement Rule

The first pilot may identify **candidate technology requirements**.

It does not authorize implementation of Institutional Memory software.

For each technology observation, record:

- manual activity;
- labor consumed;
- error or traceability risk;
- whether the task is deterministic;
- whether human judgment is essential;
- whether AI assistance might be useful;
- and whether the problem blocked or merely inconvenienced delivery.

Classify the observation as:

- **CONVENIENCE**
- **MEANINGFUL BOTTLENECK**
- **REPEATABILITY RISK**
- **DELIVERY-BLOCKING**

A candidate requirement should not become a production technology requirement
solely because it appeared once.

Validated practice must continue to drive software requirements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 21. Methodology Disposition Vocabulary

After the pilot learning review, assign one methodology disposition.

### SUPPORTS CONTROLLED REPEAT

The core method produced defensible and useful analytical output and no material
methodology defect prevents another bounded pilot.

### SUPPORTS REPEAT WITH REFINEMENT

The core method is usable, but identified minor or manageable methodology
changes should be made before or during the next controlled pilot.

### MATERIAL REDESIGN REQUIRED

The current method has defects significant enough that another pilot should not
begin in the same configuration.

### DEFINED RESEARCH REQUIRED

A specific field-derived research or falsification question must be resolved
before the affected methodology can advance.

### CURRENT METHOD NOT SUPPORTED

The pilot evidence materially fails to support continued use of the current
diagnostic configuration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 22. Commercial Outcome Vocabulary

Use the existing Pilot Authorization Package commercial outcome vocabulary.

### STRONG

Use when the engagement provides strong evidence of client value and the
approximately-$10,000 configuration operates inside sustainable delivery
economics and controlled scope.

### PROMISING

Use when the core commercial proposition is supported but one or more
manageable pricing, timing, scope, sales, or delivery conditions remain.

### REQUIRES REDESIGN

Use when client value may exist but the current scope, price, labor, delivery,
or sales configuration requires material change before another equivalent
pilot.

### COMMERCIALLY UNSUSTAINABLE AT CURRENT SCOPE / PRICE

Use when the current engagement configuration cannot reasonably be delivered
at the current scope / price relationship.

One pilot does not establish that the entire market is commercially
unsustainable.

## 24. Overall Next-Action Decision

After methodology and commercial outcomes are assigned, select one governing
next action:

- **REPEAT CONTROLLED PILOT — SAME CORE CONFIGURATION**
- **REPEAT CONTROLLED PILOT — WITH GOVERNED REFINEMENTS**
- **REDESIGN BEFORE NEXT PILOT**
- **OPEN DEFINED RESEARCH REQUIREMENT**
- **STOP CURRENT COMMERCIAL CONFIGURATION**

Do not collapse the underlying methodology and commercial evidence into one
score.

The rationale shall identify which evidence produced the decision.

## 26. Exact Use of This Framework

If a pilot prospect is later selected and authorized:

1. preserve this framework as the pre-pilot validation baseline;
2. begin the existing Pilot Measurement Record at engagement authorization;
3. record qualification, sales, scope, delivery, methodology, client-value,
   economic, repeatability, research, and technology evidence during delivery;
4. conduct the Pilot Learning Review separately from the client deliverable;
5. assign the methodology disposition;
6. assign the commercial outcome;
7. assign the governing next action;
8. record any methodology, research, pricing, sales, repeatability, or
   technology changes as evidence-driven post-pilot decisions.

If no current prospect is selected, preserve this framework unchanged for the
next governed pilot candidate.
