# 1. Purpose

The Institutional Memory Diagnostic is the first directly monetizable
application of Research Program 001.

Its purpose is to determine whether an organization possesses materially
relevant knowledge but fails to convert that knowledge reliably into effective
decision availability, escalation, corrective action, execution,
verification, reassessment, or operational control.

The Diagnostic must produce evidence-backed organizational findings rather
than generic consulting observations.

The central commercial test is:

> Can The Russow Institute sell and competently perform a $10,000
> Institutional Memory Diagnostic using the intellectual property that exists
> today?

# 3. Diagnostic Objective

The Diagnostic evaluates how consequential knowledge actually moves through
an organization.

It seeks to determine:

1. what the organization knows;
2. where that knowledge resides;
3. whether its meaning and significance are preserved;
4. whether it reaches appropriate authority;
5. whether its significance is recognized;
6. whether escalation occurs when required;
7. whether recognized knowledge becomes corrective action;
8. whether authorized action is executed;
9. whether effectiveness is verified;
10. whether experience causes reassessment and learning;
11. whether formal systems function effectively in practice;
12. whether organizational capacity interferes with conversion.

The objective is not merely to locate missing information.

The objective is to identify specific organizational conversion failures.

# 5. Diagnostic Domains

## D1 — Knowledge Existence

Determine whether materially relevant knowledge exists somewhere within or
available to the organization.

## D2 — Institutional Possession

Determine where knowledge is stored, documented, remembered, or embodied and
whether the organization possesses it beyond dependence on one individual.

## D3 — Meaning Preservation

Determine whether severity, uncertainty, context, and operational significance
remain attached to information as it moves through organizational systems.

## D4 — Decision Availability

Determine whether relevant information reaches the people or systems with
authority to act.

## D5 — Recognition and Interpretation

Determine whether receiving authorities understand why the information
matters.

## D6 — Escalation

Determine what causes issues to move to higher levels of authority and what
prevents or weakens escalation.

## D7 — Corrective Conversion

Determine whether recognized knowledge produces concrete corrective action.

## D8 — Execution

Determine whether authorized corrective action is actually performed.

## D9 — Verification

Determine whether the organization verifies that corrective action solved the
underlying problem rather than merely closing an item.

## D10 — Learning / Reassessment

Determine whether repeated abnormalities, incidents, near misses, failed
corrections, or recurring problems trigger reassessment of the underlying
system.

## D11 — Formal-System Effectiveness

Determine whether formal systems such as audits, training, safety, quality,
maintenance, reporting, management of change, and assurance function
effectively in practice.

## D12 — Organizational Capacity

Determine whether schedule, staffing, workload, budget, production pressure,
leadership turnover, organizational instability, or related constraints reduce
the organization's capacity to convert knowledge into action.

# 6. Target Client

The initial target client should be an organization with sufficient operating
history and documentary evidence to permit examination of organizational
knowledge flows.

Potential qualifying characteristics include:

- repeated operational problems;
- recurring customer complaints;
- repeated quality defects;
- safety incidents or near misses;
- corrective actions that recur after closure;
- audit findings that repeatedly reappear;
- substantial employee turnover;
- leadership transition;
- fragmented documentation;
- multiple departments handling related problems independently;
- difficulty converting frontline knowledge into management action;
