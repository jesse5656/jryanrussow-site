# Research Question

What does the BP Texas City case demonstrate about an organization's ability
to preserve, transmit, interpret, and act on knowledge relevant to catastrophic
risk?

---

# Established BP Findings

## BF-01 — Material Process-Safety Knowledge Existed Before the Explosion

Before March 23, 2005, BP Texas City and BP organizational systems possessed
substantial documented knowledge of process-safety hazards relevant to the
event, including recurring blowdown releases, abnormal splitter startups,
instrument problems, mechanical-integrity deficiencies, staffing concerns,
training deficiencies, organizational instability, safety-culture problems,
and weaknesses identified through audits and assessments.

Institutional possession was distributed and uneven; this finding does not
assert that every individual possessed every relevant fact.

**Classification:** Direct historical / organizational knowledge

---

## BF-02 — Known Safety Information Did Not Reliably Produce Corrective Action

Across multiple systems, recognized hazards and identified deficiencies did
not reliably produce timely, completed, and effective corrective action.

The record includes unresolved investigation actions, incomplete PSM action
items, uncorrected instrumentation problems, repeated blowdown events,
deferred maintenance, incomplete training, and known hazards that remained in
service.

The corpus also documents genuine corrective initiatives, so this finding does
not mean BP took no corrective action.

**Classification:** Organizational process

---

## BF-03 — Incident Reporting and Learning Did Not Preserve a Complete Operational Risk History

BP Texas City's reporting and learning architecture did not reliably capture,
integrate, investigate, and retain the significance of recurring process-safety
events.

The CSB reconstructed significant prior blowdown history from fragmented
sources because no single reporting system contained the complete record.
Several serious events were captured only as environmental events, some were
absent from incident databases, and only a minority received safety
investigations.

**Classification:** Organizational process / institutional memory

---

## BF-04 — Personal-Safety Performance Did Not Provide an Adequate View of Process-Safety Risk

BP Texas City achieved improving personal-injury performance while serious
process-safety conditions persisted.

Management attention, performance measures, campaigns, and rewards placed
substantial emphasis on personal safety.

Low injury rates therefore did not provide an adequate picture of catastrophic
process-safety risk.

**Classification:** Organizational measurement / risk representation

---

## BF-05 — Mechanical-Integrity Systems Did Not Reliably Restore Known Safety-Critical Equipment

Known problems with safety-critical instruments and equipment persisted into
the March 23 startup.

Evidence includes stale or incorrect calibration information, incomplete
repair, work-order closure despite unfinished repairs, ineffective functional
testing, nonoperational equipment, and known instrument impairment before
startup.

Failure certainty differs among individual instruments and remains qualified
in the atomic ledger.

**Classification:** Direct historical / mechanical integrity

---

## BF-06 — Hazardous Startup Was Not Supported by Sufficiently Reliable Training, Staffing, and Technical Assistance

The startup occurred within a system in which training, staffing, fatigue
control, role documentation, and technically experienced assistance were not
sufficiently reliable for a hazardous abnormal operating state.

Earlier staffing warnings, incomplete and stale training, limited
abnormal-situation preparation, extended work schedules, and competing duties
affecting experienced startup support are all represented in the evidence.

This finding does not claim that operators received no training and does not
reduce the event to operator error.

**Classification:** Organizational capability / human factors

---

## BF-07 — Management of Change Did Not Reliably Control Technical and Organizational Change

BP Texas City had a formal Management-of-Change system, but the evidence shows
repeated failures to invoke, apply, review, or close MOC appropriately for
equipment, procedures, staffing, organizational arrangements, and turnaround
siting.

The finding concerns ineffective implementation rather than absence of a
formal policy.

**Classification:** Organizational process / change control

---

## BF-08 — Resource, Production, and Organizational Pressures Reduced Process-Safety Capacity

Budget pressure, maintenance spending constraints, staffing pressure,
production demands, leadership turnover, and repeated organizational
restructuring reduced available process-safety capacity at Texas City.

The evidence also documents management resistance to some cuts and genuine
safety-improvement efforts.

This finding is therefore contextual and does not establish cost cutting as a
sole cause.

**Classification:** Contextual / organizational capacity

---

## BF-09 — Leadership and Oversight Responses Were Insufficient Relative to the Warning Record

BP site management, senior refining leadership, and corporate oversight
systems received repeated indications of significant process-safety
weaknesses before the explosion.

The organization undertook genuine responses, but the aggregate response did
not correct the major underlying deficiencies before March 23, 2005.

Board, senior-executive, and site-management responsibility remain distinct.

**Classification:** Organizational oversight

---

## BF-10 — Repeated Abnormal Outcomes Became Insufficient Triggers for System Reassessment

Recurring abnormal startups, relief events, vapor releases, equipment
malfunctions, and prior incidents did not consistently trigger reassessment of
the underlying system design, operating assumptions, instrumentation,
procedures, or risk controls.

Successful continuation of operations coexisted with repeated abnormal
conditions.

This finding does not assert that every abnormal event was identical to the
March 23 event.

**Classification:** Organizational learning / normalization of deviation

---

# Cross-Cutting Qualifications

- Formal safety-management systems existed.
- Genuine corrective and improvement initiatives occurred.
- Institutional possession does not imply uniform individual awareness.
- CSB retrospective causal conclusions remain distinguished from
  contemporaneous organizational knowledge.
- Instrument-failure certainty varies by instrument.
- Fatigue remains a likely contributing condition rather than a deterministic
  explanation.
- Resource pressure is not treated as a sole cause.
- Operator training existed.
- Organizational levels are not collapsed.
- These findings are BP Texas City case findings, not universal propositions.
