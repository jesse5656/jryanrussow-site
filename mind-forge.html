The Golden Rule: Repair vs. Replace

The decision comes down to three factors: the age of the roof, the extent of the damage, and your long-term financial goals.

✓ The Case for REPAIR

Best for isolated, minor issues on a relatively young roof. It is a short-term fix to extend the life of your current system.

  • Lower immediate out-of-pocket cost
  • Quick turnaround (often same-day)
  • Ideal for localized leaks or missing shingles
  • Preserves the remaining lifespan of the roof

✕ The Case for REPLACEMENT

Best for old roofs, widespread damage, or structural issues. It is a long-term investment that protects the entire home.

  • Eliminates recurring repair costs
  • Boosts home value and curb appeal
  • Unlocks new manufacturer warranties
  • Improves energy efficiency and insulation

The 50% Rule of Thumb:

If the cost of the repair is more than 50% of the cost of a replacement, or if the roof is past 75% of its expected lifespan, replacement is almost always the smarter financial choice.

When You Should Repair

Not every issue requires a tear-off. A targeted repair is the right move if your roof meets the following criteria.

Signs a Repair is Enough:

  • The roof is relatively new: Less than 10-15 years old on a 25-30 year architectural shingle.
  • Damage is highly localized: A single fallen branch, a few missing shingles, or a small leak around a plumbing vent.
  • Flashing issues only: Leaks around chimneys or skylights can often be fixed by resealing or replacing the flashing without touching the shingles.
  • No decking damage: The underlying wood (decking) is completely dry and structurally sound.

✓ Pro Tip:

If you choose to repair, ensure the contractor uses matching materials. Patching an old roof with brand-new shingles can create weak points and hurt your home's curb appeal.

When You Must Replace

Throwing good money after bad repairs is a common homeowner mistake. You need a full replacement if you see these red flags.

Age & Lifespan

If your asphalt shingle roof is over 20 years old, it is at the end of its useful life. Frequent repairs will cost more than a replacement over the next few years.

Widespread Shingle Damage

Curling, cupping, clawing, or massive granule loss across multiple roof faces indicates systemic failure, not just isolated wear.

Sagging or Decking Rot

A sagging roofline or spongy-feeling decking means the structural wood is rotting. This is a major safety hazard requiring a full tear-off.

Multiple or Recurring Leaks

If you are patching leaks in different areas every year, the underlayment has failed. A replacement is the only permanent fix.

Daylight in the Attic

If you go into your attic and see beams of sunlight through the roof boards, your decking is compromised.

Planning to Sell Soon

A new roof yields a high return on investment. Buyers and inspectors will flag an old roof, derailing your sale or forcing a price drop.

⚠️ Warning:

Never install a new roof over an existing layer of damaged shingles ("roofing over"). This traps moisture, voids manufacturer warranties, and hides rotten decking. Always demand a full tear-off.

The Storm Damage & Insurance Factor

In Southwest Missouri and Northwest Arkansas, severe weather is a fact of life. Storm damage changes the repair vs. replace math entirely.

1

Hail & Wind Damage

If a storm causes widespread functional damage (bruised matting, torn shingles), your homeowner's insurance will typically cover a full replacement, minus your deductible. Do not pay out-of-pocket for storm damage.

2

The Insurance "Age & Condition" Clause

If your roof was already near the end of its life before the storm, insurance may only pay for a "prorated" cash value repair rather than a full replacement. A professional inspection is required to prove the storm caused the failure.

3

Matching Shingle Availability

If your roof is older, the original shingles may be discontinued. Insurance codes often mandate a full replacement if a patch job would result in a mismatched, un-uniform roof appearance.

Cost Comparison & Long-Term ROI

The cheapest option today is not always the cheapest option tomorrow. Here is how the costs break down.

Roof Repair Economics:

  • Upfront Cost: Low ($300 - $1,500 typically).
  • Lifespan Extension: Adds a few months to a few years.
  • Hidden Risk: If the underlying issue isn't fully diagnosed, you will pay for a second repair later.

Roof Replacement Economics:

  • Upfront Cost: High ($8,000 - $20,000+ depending on size/pitch).
  • Lifespan Extension: 25 to 50+ years of total protection.
  • ROI Benefits: Increases home resale value, lowers home insurance premiums, and improves energy efficiency.
  • Peace of Mind: Backed by 30-to-50-year manufacturer and workmanship warranties.

Questions to Ask Your Contractor

Before you sign a check for a repair or a replacement, ask your roofing contractor these critical questions.

Can this issue be fixed with a localized repair?

A good contractor will give you an honest assessment. If they push for a full replacement when a $400 flashing repair will stop the leak, find a new contractor.

What is the condition of my roof decking?

They cannot know this 100% until the old roof is removed, but they should check the attic for soft spots, moisture, or sagging before giving a recommendation.

Is this damage covered by my homeowner's insurance?

If the damage is from a recent storm (hail, wind, falling tree), a reputable contractor will help you navigate the insurance claim process rather than billing you directly.

Will a repair void my existing manufacturer warranty?

Sometimes, unauthorized repairs or using incompatible materials can void your remaining warranty. Always check first.

What warranties come with a full replacement?

Ensure you are getting both the manufacturer's material warranty and the contractor's workmanship warranty in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most residential roof replacements in the Joplin and Springfield areas are completed in just 1 to 3 days, depending on the size of the home, roof pitch, and weather conditions.

Can I get a roof replacement in the winter?

Yes. Asphalt shingles can be installed in cold weather as long as the temperature is above freezing and the decking is dry. In fact, winter is often a great time to schedule a replacement to beat the spring storm rush.

Do I need to leave my house during the replacement?

No, but it will be loud. The vibration from the tear-off can shake items off your walls. We recommend moving fragile items away from walls and keeping pets in a secure, quiet room or off-site for the day.

What if you find rotten wood when you take the old roof off?

This is common. Reputable contractors include a "wood rot replacement" clause in the estimate. If rotten decking is found, we will show you, quote a per-sheet replacement price, and fix it before laying the new underlayment.

Will a new roof lower my home insurance premium?

Often, yes. Installing a new roof — especially impact-resistant or wind-rated shingles — signals to insurers that your home is at a lower risk for claims, which can result in a discount on your premium.
